## About

Maple Helper is a desktop assistant for Windows and macOS that puts a chat overlay on top of MapleStory Classic World. Open it from the Maple Helper icon by the clock (the menu bar on macOS), then type a question or click the microphone to speak (click again to stop). Answers come from your own AI account (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Z.AI or Muse Spark) and draw on a screenshot of your game, your character profile, previous conversations, and a local copy of the NiaMeowDB game database.

Use it to look up drops, find quest NPCs, or ask where to train without switching away from the game. A **toolbar over the game** adds tools that read the same database, each in its own window: where to train, farming, quests for your level, citizenship, your build and skills, gear upgrades, scrolls, crafting and the Cash Shop, with your hit and damage on any monster's page.

Website: **https://maplehelper.app/** (source: [maple-helper-site](https://github.com/Maple-Helper/maple-helper-site))

## Features

- **In-game chat:** Open the overlay from the tray icon, the desktop shortcut or the taskbar, and resize it from any edge or corner.
- **Your choice of AI:** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Z.AI or Muse Spark, on your own account or an API key. The app installs each one's official tool for you, with the progress shown in the app, and you can switch any time in settings.
- **Stays on the game:** Questions that aren't about MapleStory Classic get a short note instead of an answer, and the answer comes in the language you asked in.
- **Screen context:** Capture the game window when you open the overlay, with a camera button to refresh the screenshot.
- **Character profiles:** Track your level, job, map, and active quests, with updates based on your conversations and screenshots. Edit, delete or switch characters from the character card's menu.
- **Conversation memory:** Keep separate chat history and session summaries for each character.
- **Visual reference cards:** See images of relevant monsters, items, maps, NPCs, and quests alongside answers, grouped under titles (for example, what a monster drops).
- **Where it is on the map:** The ◎ on a map, NPC or quest (on its card, or its tile under "Maps", "NPCs" or "Quests") opens a window beside the chat. A map shows the map next door with an orange dot on the portal that leads in, as NiaMeowDB marks it ("Leads back here"); an NPC, its map with a green dot where it stands (one inside a shop: the shop's door); a quest, where to start it and where to turn it in. The window reopens wherever you last left it.
- **Where you are, from the minimap:** Maple Helper finds the game's MINI MAP window at the game's top left on its own (keep it open there) and reads it every second (Settings → Minimap → "How often to read the minimap": half a second, 1, 2, 5 or 10 seconds), reading the map you're in from the minimap's title, and shows it under your level. While it knows where you are, the ◎ window draws the way there from your map, step by step with your blue dot on the first map (a legend tells it from the portal's orange one), and so does a map's page in the toolbar's Maps search. The way is on foot unless "Cabs & Teleports" is on in the toolbar's settings (its gear button), which adds cabs and NPC teleports. It takes an invisible teleport only where nothing else gets there, such as a shop's exit.
- **Hidden portals on the minimap:** A small blue dot on the game's own minimap marks each invisible teleport: a portal the game never draws, which you take by pressing up on its spot or walking into it. The dots move with the minimap as it scrolls; turn them off in the toolbar's settings.
- **Route arrow on the minimap:** Open a map in the toolbar's Maps search, or a ◎ window, and an arrow on the game's own minimap points at the next portal or NPC on the way there. The minimap often shows only part of the map; when that step is off the part shown, a bigger arrow at the minimap's edge points the way to it. A small name beside the arrow says what it points at: the map the portal leads to, a hidden teleport, or the NPC to talk to. Reaching the map turns the arrow off and says you're there, and closing the page or ◎ window that lit it turns it off as well. Its colour, size and opacity, the name beside it and that name's own opacity, whether it flashes and the notice on arriving are all set in the toolbar's settings, where you can also turn the arrow off. The arrow's opacity fades the arrow and the "maps to go" strip together, down to a fifth of solid, so the way can stay on screen over a minimap it would otherwise cover; the name beside it has an opacity of its own over the same range, so a faint arrow can keep a name that still reads, or a solid one a name that hides less of the map. The hidden portals' blue dots and the guided NPC's ring are never faded.
- **Guided the whole way:** The arrow doesn't stop at the map it was lit on. The way is worked out from wherever you stand, so each new map the app reads re-points it; take the wrong portal and it says you're off the way and works it out again from where you are; and the map you picked is kept until you arrive. Under the minimap, outside the picture, it says how many maps are still to go (and that this is the last one). Turn the guiding off ("Guide the whole way") and the arrow leads on the map it was lit from alone; turn the count off ("How many maps are left") and nothing is said under the minimap.
- **Teleport arrows on the app's map:** In Maple Helper's own map window, every hidden teleport on the map is drawn as an arrow from its spot to where it lands, with a legend for it. Spots that land in the same place are gathered into one arrow marked with how many there are. Turn it off with "Teleport arrows on Maple Helper's map".
- **Tag cards:** Tap one or more cards to ask follow-up questions about them, such as "where is it?" or "which one is easier for me?".
- **Who drops it:** Ask which monsters drop an item and get the answer grouped by monster, with pictures.
- **Item details:** The ⓘ on an item's card or tile opens a window to the right of the chat with all the item's stats from its NiaMeowDB page, the monsters that drop it (with their level, map and an "Ask in chat" button), and NiaMeowDB's "Meow Notes". The window reopens wherever you last left it.
- **Guides library:** 32 full guides (every class, grind maps, the EXP table, the damage formula and more) with skill and item icons, character art, tables and tips, in English and Hebrew. The ones that fit your character come first.
- **Plan usage:** See how much of your Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Z.AI or Muse Spark plan is used, with a heads-up in the chat before the 5-hour limit runs out, and a saver mode.
- **Tools over the game:** The game controller button in the chat shows or hides a toolbar over the game. Its Tools card opens each tool in its own window, its searches open a monster's, a map's or an item's page, and its Loot Log button the loot log; all built from the game database and your character, and linked to each other (a monster's way there and its hit & damage, an item's droppers and prices):
  - **Grind spots:** the monsters to grind at your level, ranked by the EXP each of your swings earns with the ACC and damage you type (or read off a screenshot), each with its map, hit chance, hits to kill, EXP, the mesos players reported and kills to the next level.
  - **Farm:** pick an item (or one you starred) to see who drops it, where and how each sits against your level; your own drop rates per hour from the loot log; and what's worth farming at your level: monsters that drop what your quests, recipes and starred items need come first, then what an NPC pays most for.
  - **Quests:** by level (this level, skipped, coming up, later, each tab with its count), a full card for each quest (where to get it, what to do, what to bring and who drops it, who to finish with, the rewards), daily and weekly quests that come back, and a search through every quest at once. **Quest chains:** each card shows the questline it is on ("Step 8 of 11 · Next: …"), opening to every step done, open or locked and why; the Chains tab lists the lines you are on with the EXP left and the next step's card, then the ones to start; marking a step done offers to mark the steps before it.
  - **Citizenship:** the Henesys and Kerning City quests by citizenship grade, the town the guide recommends for your class and why, and where to sign up.
  - **Build & skills:** your class guide's AP, SP and gear tables, your level's row highlighted, a table for each 2nd job, and the community tier list for your job; the Skills tab has every class line's skill books, each skill with what it does at level 1 and at its max level.
  - **Gear:** your equip slots laid out like the game's equip window, each counting the upgrades you can wear now; a slot's upgrades as cards, best first, with their stat changes and where to get them (an NPC's price, the Free Market's usual price, the monsters that drop it), and the ones a few levels away.
  - **Scrolls:** pick a piece of gear, the stat to scroll and how many of its slots to use: which grade is the safe pick, what each grade adds on average and how rarely every slot passes, the odds of each final total, and the cost.
  - **Crafting:** what your class can craft, with the cost from raw materials and what crafting saves against the NPC's price, and a Forge plan beside it (what to farm first and where it drops, then the crafts in order). The Professions tab shows each profession's level and the EXP to the next, what that level opens, every recipe by level, searchable by name or ingredient, and its teacher, where to craft and its quests.
  - **Cash shop:** what the game's Cash Shop sells right now and for how many NX, in the game's own tabs (Special, Fashion, Beauty, Pets, Utility) and their sections, each item with what it does and how long it lasts, and what the Founder's Mystery Fashion Crate can give with each item's chance. From the game client's own list (OSMS Data Explorer) and MesoWatch, updating by itself.
  - **Pets** (the Cash shop's Pets tab): lifespan, hunger, commands to level 30 and the fastest command at each stage, Cash Shop prices, and the pet skills.
  - **Hit & damage:** on a monster's page in the toolbar's Monsters & NPCs search: your hit chance and the ACC you need to never miss, your damage on it and the hits to kill, the EXP per kill and kills to the next level, and the mesos players reported.
  - **Free Market details:** from an item's page in the toolbar's Items search, its full card in a side window: the usual sale price (the median), the last sale, the last 3 sales' average, where to buy it (the shops seen selling it, cheapest first, with the owner's character) and the newest sales.
  - **Loot log:** a small see-through window over the game: press Start and every "You received EXP", "You have gained mesos" and "x1 earned" message is counted on your computer, each one once: EXP, mesos and what an NPC pays for the items, each per hour, every item picked up and the latest pickups. The messages are found on their own at the game window's bottom right, at any resolution or window size. Every log and pickup is also kept for good in a database on your computer (`loot.sqlite` in the app's data folder), with the time and what an NPC paid for each item, so any day can be looked back on. No AI and no screenshots sent anywhere.
- **Toolbar settings:** The toolbar's gear button opens a small panel whose choices apply at once. It sets the look of every window over the game (the glass colour, the accent colour, how see-through the glass is, and icons instead of words on the toolbar's buttons) and the key that shows or hides the toolbar (Shift+F11 by default, or Shift+F10 on macOS). Its Directions section has "Cabs & Teleports"; its Navigation section has the four switches "Guide the whole way" (`minimap_route_follow`), "How many maps are left" (`minimap_route_steps`), "Say when you arrive" (`minimap_arrival_notice`) and "Teleport arrows on Maple Helper's map" (`map_teleport_arrows`), all on to begin with; and its Minimap section has "Hidden portals on the minimap", "Route arrow on the minimap" with the arrow's colour, its size and its opacity on sliders (`minimap_arrow_size`, `minimap_arrow_opacity`), the name beside it with its own opacity slider (`minimap_arrow_label_opacity`) and whether it flashes, and "Show the overlays in screenshots" (Windows). That last one is off by default, because the minimap reads and the game shots the AI gets would see the overlays too; turn it on to capture and share them. Settings → Minimap keeps how often the minimap is read, the toolbar itself (on or off) and its key.
- **News:** MapleStory Classic news with the full articles in the app, in English and Hebrew.
- **Sources on every number:** a tag says where each value comes from (community reports, the COT1 / COT2 test builds, MSEA, or official game data once it's out).
- **Game terms explained:** A **?** in your accent colour beside terms like ACC, Avoid or AP shows what they mean on hover.
- **Tracked items:** Star an item to keep a list of what you hunt, with who drops it and where.
- **Hebrew and English:** Use either language, including mixed text with English game names.
- **Local speech recognition:** Transcribe voice input on your computer using ivrit.ai Whisper models.

The app uses screen capture and its own overlay window. It does not read game memory, inject input, or automate gameplay.

## Requirements

- Windows 10 or 11, or macOS 14 or later on Apple Silicon (M1 or newer).
- Python 3.10 or later to run from source (the installer needs no Python).
- One AI provider, chosen during setup and switchable in settings:
  - **Claude**: Claude Code installed, with a Claude Pro or Max account or an Anthropic API key.
  - **ChatGPT**: a paid ChatGPT plan or an OpenAI API key (the app installs and signs in to OpenAI's official tool, the Codex CLI, for you). ChatGPT answers appear all at once instead of streaming.
  - **Gemini**: a personal Google account or a Gemini API key. The app installs Google's official tool, Antigravity (since June 2026 Google serves personal accounts only through it), and signs you in: after signing in to Google in the browser, you paste the code it shows into the app, within a minute.
  - **Grok**: a Grok account (a free one works, with a limited quota; SuperGrok or X Premium+ gives more), or an xAI API key. The app installs xAI's official tool, Grok Build, and signs you in: you approve the sign-in in the browser (the code is filled in).
  - **Z.AI**: your Z.AI API key (a GLM Coding Plan or Z.AI API credit), answering with the GLM-5.3 model. The app installs Oh My Pi, the open-source AI coding CLI it answers through. There is no account sign-in: you paste the key in the setup or in Settings, or, if you already gave Oh My Pi a Z.AI key, the app uses that one. GLM-5.3 reads text only, so Z.AI's vision model GLM-5.3-Flash (on the same key) describes the game screenshot for it.
  - **Muse Spark**: a Muse Code plan from Meta, answering with Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor, or a Meta Model API key with billing set up. When Meta's servers for 1.3 are overloaded, the answer comes from Muse Spark 1.2 Contributor on the same plan instead. The app installs Oh My Pi and signs you in: you approve the sign-in in the browser (the code is filled in). If you are already signed in to Muse Code in Oh My Pi, the app uses that sign-in.
- MapleStory Classic World running in **Borderless** or **Windowed Fullscreen** mode.
- An internet connection for AI responses and initial data downloads.
- A microphone if you want to use voice input.

## Install

Download the latest release from the [Releases page](https://github.com/abilliontokens/maple-helper/releases/latest):

- **`MapleHelper-Setup.exe`**: the installer. This is the recommended option and doesn't need admin rights.
- **`MapleHelper-<version>-portable.zip`**: unzip it anywhere and run `Maple Helper.exe`.
- **`MapleHelper-macOS.dmg`**: for Macs with Apple Silicon. Open it and drag **Maple Helper** into Applications.

All of them include the knowledge base. The installed Windows app updates itself; the portable zip and the Mac app tell you when a new version is out.

Windows may show **Windows protected your PC** the first time, because the app is not code-signed yet: click **More info → Run anyway**.

### First launch on macOS

The Mac app is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway** next to Maple Helper. You do this once per version.

Maple Helper lives in the menu bar (there is no Dock icon). Allow **Screen Recording** when it asks (or under **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording**), then restart the app: that is how it sees the game window. On macOS 15 the setting is called **Screen & System Audio Recording**.

After an update, screenshots can stop working although Maple Helper still shows as allowed: the new version needs the permission again. Select Maple Helper in that list, remove it with **−**, add it again (or turn its switch off and on), then restart the app.

macOS may also ask you from time to time to confirm that Maple Helper can keep recording your screen. Choose **Allow**, or it can't see the game.

MapleStory Classic World runs on Mac too. Maple Helper finds the game window by its title.

On most Mac keyboards, the F-keys control brightness and volume. Hold **fn** while pressing them, or turn on **System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Function Keys → Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys**.

## Run from source

Open PowerShell (Windows) or Terminal (macOS) in the repository folder. On macOS, use `.venv/bin/python` instead of `.\.venv\Scripts\python`, and `/` instead of `\` in paths.

1. Create a virtual environment and install the dependencies:

   ```powershell
   python -m venv .venv
   .\.venv\Scripts\python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
   ```

2. Download the knowledge base:

   ```powershell
   .\.venv\Scripts\python tools\scrape_meowdb.py
   ```

   This saves game data and images to `data/kb/`. The initial download can take a while. If interrupted, run the command again to resume.

3. Start the app:

   ```powershell
   .\.venv\Scripts\python -m maplehelper
   ```

4. Follow the setup screens to choose a language, connect your AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Z.AI or Muse Spark), and create your character profile. For each provider, the setup can install its CLI and sign you in, or take an API key (Anthropic for Claude, OpenAI for ChatGPT, Google AI Studio for Gemini, xAI for Grok, Z.AI for Z.AI, Meta for Muse Spark).

## Using the overlay

| Control | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Tray icon** | Click the Maple Helper icon by the clock (the menu bar on macOS) to open the chat; in a full-screen game, the Windows key shows it. |
| **Microphone button** | Start recording your voice; click again to transcribe and, by default, send the question. |
| **Window buttons** | Minimize the overlay to the taskbar or close it (the tray icon opens it again). |
| **Tap a card** | Tag it for the next question; tap again to untag. |
| **Camera button** | Take a fresh screenshot of the game window. |
| **Game controller button** | Show or hide the toolbar over the game (lit while it is up); its Tools card opens Grind spots, Farm, Quests, Citizenship, Build & skills, Gear, Scrolls, Crafting and the Cash shop, each in its own window over the game, and its gear button the toolbar's settings. |
| **Character card** | Open its menu to switch, edit, delete, add or copy characters; **What now?** asks for the best next step. |
| **Accent-coloured ?** | Hover it to see what a game term means. |
| **System tray menu** | Show the overlay, show or hide the toolbar, open settings, or quit the app. |

In settings, you can change the AI provider (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Z.AI or Muse Spark) and its account, the look (**Settings > Appearance**: the glass colour (Dark, Midnight blue, Forest, Wine, Slate or White), the accent colour, and the opacity), language, font size, answer length, starting with Windows, and whether voice questions are sent immediately. The windows over the game keep a look of their own, set from the toolbar's gear button, which also holds the toolbar's key and the switches for the way and the minimap, all applied at once. **Settings > Minimap** keeps how often the minimap is read, the toolbar itself and its key.

The speech model downloads on first use, so the first voice request takes longer. Transcription uses CUDA when available and falls back to the CPU (always the CPU on macOS).

## Data and privacy

Settings, character profiles, conversation history, speech models, and downloaded knowledge-base updates are stored locally in `%APPDATA%\MapleHelper` on Windows and `~/Library/Application Support/MapleHelper` on macOS. The knowledge base built from source is stored in `data/kb/` inside the repository. API keys entered in the app are stored in Windows Credential Manager or the macOS Keychain. Antigravity (Gemini) and Grok Build run with folders of their own inside Maple Helper's data folder, so your own setup of those tools never reaches the answers; Grok's sign-in is kept there, and Antigravity's in the system credential store it shares with your own Antigravity. Oh My Pi (Z.AI and Muse Spark) runs with a folder of its own inside Maple Helper's data folder too, where a sign-in made in the app is kept. When the app has no Z.AI key or Muse Spark sign-in of its own but your own Oh My Pi does, the answers run on your Oh My Pi's sign-in, with Maple Helper's settings laid over yours: your MCP servers, plugins, extensions, skills, rules, memory, advisor and shell are all off, and only the knowledge base can be read. Signing out of that sign-in is done in Oh My Pi itself.

When you ask a question, the app sends your chosen AI provider (Anthropic for Claude, OpenAI for ChatGPT, Google for Gemini, xAI for Grok, Z.AI for Z.AI, Meta for Muse Spark) your question, the available game screenshot, character profile, recent conversation, earlier session summaries, and relevant knowledge-base context. The AI can also read local knowledge-base files to answer the question; it runs read-only and cannot change files or search the web. Claude, Grok, Z.AI and Muse Spark can read nothing outside the knowledge base and the screenshot; Gemini is kept out of your own files too. With Codex, Gemini, Grok, Z.AI and Muse Spark, the screenshot is written to a temporary file for the run and deleted right after. After a chat session has been closed for 30 minutes, the app may send the session transcript to the same provider to generate a summary for future conversations.

Voice recordings are transcribed locally. The resulting text is used as your question. The Free Market details window reads MesoWatch's public market file and its sellers' character pictures (nothing is sent; checked every 15 minutes while the app is open, downloaded compressed only when it changed, kept on disk). The app also checks GitHub Releases for app and knowledge-base updates, and installs an update only when its SHA-256 matches the release's checksums.

So we know how many people use Maple Helper, every 10 minutes while it runs the app tells our PostHog project that it is running: its version, your OS, and whether this is the first time ever, today or this month. The id it is sent under is random for each run and never stored, so runs can't be linked to each other or to you, and the project discards IP addresses. Turn it off in **Settings → Privacy & system → Count me as a player**. Separately, **Share anonymous usage stats** (on unless you turn it off) also sends which features get used, under a random id kept on your computer. Neither ever sends your questions, answers, screenshots, voice or character names. Setting the environment variable `MAPLEHELPER_NO_TELEMETRY=1` turns both off.

## Development

Install the development tools, then run the linter and the tests (CI runs the same on every pull request):

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m ruff check .
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m pytest tests -q
```

Changes go through a branch and a pull request; `main` accepts a merge only when CI (lint, tests, Windows build and installer round trip) is green. Releases are described in [docs/RELEASING.md](docs/RELEASING.md).

For a small knowledge-base download during development, limit the scraper to five pages per category:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python tools\scrape_meowdb.py --limit 5
```
